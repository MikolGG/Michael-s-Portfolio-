The problem is trust, not just price.

Problem

Small-business owners manage orders manually and deal with unreliable delivery, little rider communication and poor shipment visibility. That creates customer anxiety, complaints and extra work.

Goal

Design a simple, trustworthy delivery experience that gives businesses clearer control over orders and gives customers visibility throughout the delivery.

What I assumed

Delivery cost was the main struggle.

Users would be fairly tech savvy.

What 15 interviews and observations showed

Reliability, real-time tracking and rider communication mattered more than cost.

Many people, especially market traders, wanted simple interfaces, voice features and local-language support.

Unreliable delivery

Late or missed deliveries mean lost sales and complaints.

Design response: real-time tracking, delivery confirmation and a rider reliability score.

No communication

Users can't reach riders or get updates, which creates anxiety and mistrust.

Design response: in-app chat and calls, automated status updates.

Cluttered interfaces

Market traders find existing logistics apps confusing.

Design response: large icons, minimal text and optional voice navigation.

Manual order handling

Sellers juggle orders, delivery details and customer info separately.

Design response: one dashboard with order syncing from Instagram and WhatsApp.

From paper sketches to a dispatch flow.

I explored five home-screen layouts on paper, keeping primary actions visible and cutting the steps needed to create or monitor a delivery.

Testing changed two screens.

Round 1 flagged slow multi-line labels, easy-to-miss edit links and hidden fares. Round 2 asked for shorter labels, stronger field hierarchy and fare plus arrival time shown together before booking.

These are findings and design responses from prototype testing, not measured results from a live product.

The high-fidelity app.

Teal for action, steel blue for text, large rounded cards and a persistent bottom bar. The Move, Track, Deliver tagline sits on every entry point.

Screen readers

Concise descriptions so people with visual impairments get context for charts and complex information.

Large touch targets

Simpler buttons and bigger tap areas reduce mis-taps for people with fine-motor limitations.

Clear errors

Specific instructions with text and an icon, to help people with cognitive disabilities finish tasks.

What I learned, and what's next.

Challenging my first assumption mattered most. Cost was a concern, but reliability and communication were the deeper trust problems, and designing around them gave the product a clearer direction.

  1. Pilot beta with local logistics partnersTest delivery assignment speed, live tracking accuracy and payment settlement with independent riders and couriers in Lagos.
  2. Add analytics and conversion trackingUse tools like Mixpanel or Hotjar to find drop-off between getting a quote and completed payment.
  3. Design driver and dispatcher appsCover order acceptance, live route optimization and operations management.
